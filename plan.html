How we estimate this

These are rules of thumb, not a fitted model, and they only use what the brief already knows. Treat 70% as "likely" and 30% as "worth a look", not as a measurement.

Chance of 15 or more species at a spot. Take the species reported there over the last three days and divide by three for a rough daily count, capped at 40. Fifteen a day is even odds (50%); every four species above or below moves it 20 points. Multiply by the morning's call score over 70 (so a GO lifts the odds and a STAY IN lowers them, held between ×0.3 and ×1.3), then cut it for weather: rain 60% or more ×0.6 (40% or more ×0.8), wind 18 mph or more ×0.7 (12 or more ×0.85).

Chance of seeing one bird. Start at 55% if it was reported nearby in the last three days, 8% if not. Apply the same score and weather factors, then a seasonal factor for its group (warblers in May ×1.2, in January ×0.1; winter finches the other way round) from a small hand-written table in js/odds.js.

Nothing is shown above 95% or below 3%. Species counts and sightings come from eBird; weather from Open-Meteo.

Free mornings

Paste your calendar's private address (Google Calendar: Settings → your calendar → "Secret address in iCal format"; any .ics link works). Daybird reads the next seven days and marks which mornings, 6:30 to 8:30, are free.

What is stored: the address stays in this browser only, so you don't have to paste it again. The server reads the calendar when you tap the button, keeps nothing, and returns only busy start and end times. Sign-in is required. Repeating events: weekly repeats are understood; other patterns count once.
