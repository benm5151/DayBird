Privacy

Daybird keeps as little as it can, shows you what it has, and deletes it when you ask. Last updated [DATE].

What Daybird stores

You can use the brief, the ID helper and the sample passes without an account. Nothing leaves your browser except the requests that fetch weather and sightings. If you sign in, Daybird stores:

WhatWhy
Your sign-in emailTo send you a magic sign-in link and briefs you ask for.
Display name, short bio, favorite birdShown on your profile and next to your posts.
Optional profile photo and favorite-bird photoShown on your profile. Stored in a public photo bucket under your account id.
Life-list summaryTotals, recent lifers, lifers by month, top spots and your species names, parsed from the eBird CSV you import. The raw CSV is read in your browser and never uploaded.
Location, roundedLatitude and longitude rounded to two decimals (about a mile), so the brief covers your area without knowing your house.
Mode and levelWhere you usually bird and how long you have been at it. They shape the brief's wording. Kept in your browser; sent with a brief request only when you ask for one by email.
Feed posts, comments and likesWhat you choose to share. Posts are public to anyone using the site.
Land-pass listings and bookingsListings are public except the exact location and directions, which only the host and people who booked can read. A booking records the listing, date, seats and when you accepted the guest waiver.
Friend circlesThe circle name, who is in it, and each morning line you post. Only members of that circle can read any of it.
Emails sent on requestWhen you email yourself a brief or share one with a friend, Daybird logs that a send happened, with the recipient's address stored only as a keyed hash so the daily limits work. The address itself is not kept.
Opt-outsIf someone unsubscribes from friends' briefs, their address is stored as the same kind of hash, so it can be matched but not read.

The site also notes your choices in your own browser (see "Cookies and local storage").

Who else sees it

Daybird runs on a few outside services. Each one sees only what it needs to do its job:

  • Supabase hosts the database, sign-in and photo storage. Everything in the table above lives there.
  • Resend delivers the emails you ask Daybird to send. It sees the recipient address and the email's contents.
  • eBird / Cornell Lab of Ornithology supplies sightings. Daybird sends it your rounded location to ask what has been reported nearby. The data comes back under eBird's terms; see Terms.
  • Open-Meteo supplies weather for your rounded location.
  • Anthropic runs the photo-ID helper. When you upload a photo for an ID, the photo and your notes go to Anthropic's API for that one request. Daybird does not keep the photo.

Each of these has its own privacy policy. Daybird has no ad network, no analytics pixels, and no tracking of you across other sites.

What Daybird does not do

  • It does not sell your data, or trade it, or hand it to anyone so they can market to you.
  • It does not read your email or your eBird account. You import a CSV you download yourself.
  • It does not store exact locations of sensitive species in anything public, and it asks you not to either.

Deleting your data

Open Settings and use "Delete my account". It removes your profile, posts, comments, likes, listings, bookings, circle memberships, the circles you own, the record of briefs you sent, your photos, and the account itself, in one step. Bookings you made on other people's land are removed too; hosts are not told. The things you shared that other people copied, such as a brief you emailed to a friend, are theirs now.

You can also email [CONTACT_EMAIL] and ask. Allow a week.

Signed out, "Reset bookings and posts" in Settings clears what this browser holds. Clearing site data in your browser removes the rest.

Cookies and local storage

Daybird sets no advertising or analytics cookies. It uses your browser's local storage for your settings, your sign-in session, your local-only posts and bookings, and your eBird API key if you add one. That key never leaves your browser. The sign-in session is a token Supabase sets so you stay signed in between visits.

Age

Daybird is for people 13 and older. If you are under 13, do not create an account. If we learn that an account belongs to someone under 13, we will delete it.

Safety

Daybird is a guide, not safety advice. It reads a weather forecast and recent sightings and gives an opinion. Check conditions yourself before heading out, especially near water, ice and private land.
